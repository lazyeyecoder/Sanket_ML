## Cleaning a minor cut, scrape, or abrasion
Summary: Rinse the wound with clean running water, clean around it, and cover it.
- Wash your hands first.
- Rinse the wound under clean, running water.
- Wash the skin around the wound with mild soap, without getting soap inside the wound.
- Do not use hydrogen peroxide or iodine on the wound - they can irritate it.
- If available, put on an antibiotic ointment or plain petroleum jelly, then cover with a clean dressing.
Source: Consistent with Mayo Clinic and Red Cross minor-wound-care guidance — https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/wounds

## Signs a minor wound is becoming infected
Summary: Worsening pain, redness, pus, streaks or fever mean it needs medical care.
- Get medical care within a few hours if you see: pain getting worse a day or more after the injury, more redness, warmth or swelling, pus or foul-smelling drainage, red streaks spreading from the wound, or fever.
Source: Consistent with Mayo Clinic and Red Cross minor-wound-care guidance — https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/wounds

## Abrasions specifically
Summary: Rinse a scrape well to remove dirt, then pat dry and cover loosely.
- Rinse a scrape well to remove dirt and debris.
- Pat it dry gently - don't rub - then cover it loosely.
- See a clinician if it is large, deep, dirty or over a joint.
Source: General first-aid guidance consistent with Red Cross wound care — https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/wounds
