## Recognizing shock
Signs and symptoms of shock include: restlessness or irritability, an altered level of consciousness (confused, drowsy, or unresponsive), pale or ashen, cool, moist skin, nausea or vomiting, rapid breathing and rapid pulse, and excessive thirst. Shock can develop after significant blood loss, severe burns, or other serious injury, and can worsen quickly.
Source: American Red Cross, "Shock" — https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/shock

## First aid for shock
Call emergency services immediately if you suspect shock. Control any external bleeding first. Have the person lie flat on their back; if there is no evidence of a head, neck, back, or leg injury, raising their feet about 6 to 12 inches can help. Keep the person from getting chilled or overheated — cover them with a blanket to prevent loss of body heat, but the goal is to maintain a normal body temperature, not to overheat them. Do not give the person food or water. Continue to monitor their level of consciousness and breathing while waiting for help, since anxiety and pain can worsen shock.
Source: American Red Cross, "Shock" — https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/shock
