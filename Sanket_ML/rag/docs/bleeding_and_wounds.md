## Controlling heavy or life-threatening bleeding
Summary: Press firmly and continuously on the wound with a clean cloth.
- Press firmly and steadily on the wound with a clean dressing, gauze or the cleanest cloth you have - a clean t-shirt will do.
- If you have disposable gloves or a plastic bag, use them so you don't touch the blood directly.
- Keep pressing without stopping - don't lift the dressing to check the wound, as this can restart the bleeding.
- If blood soaks through, add more dressing on top; don't remove the first layer.
Source: American Red Cross, "Bleeding (Life-Threatening External)" — https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/bleeding-life-threatening-external

## When direct pressure is not enough
Summary: If pressure doesn't stop the bleeding, keep pressing and call emergency services.
- Keep pressing firmly and call emergency services if the bleeding does not stop.
- A tourniquet on a limb is only for someone trained to use one, placed between the wound and the heart.
- Wound packing (for the scalp, neck, shoulder, groin or back) is also for trained people only - otherwise keep firm pressure and wait for emergency help.
Source: American Red Cross, "Bleeding (Life-Threatening External)" — https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/bleeding-life-threatening-external

## Getting emergency help for bleeding [urgent]
Summary: Call emergency services for heavy bleeding and keep the person still.
- Call emergency services for bleeding that is heavy, spurting or does not slow with direct pressure, or if the person shows signs of shock.
- Keep the person as still and calm as possible while you wait.
- Do not pull out an object stuck in a wound - keep it steady and get emergency care.
Source: American Red Cross, "Bleeding (Life-Threatening External)" — https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/bleeding-life-threatening-external
